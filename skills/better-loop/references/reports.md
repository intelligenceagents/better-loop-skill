# Private reports and readable public stories

## Private assessment on the laptop

Start with one practical conclusion. Then show:

1. Scope: selected inputs, task goal, platform(s), and missing evidence.
2. What worked: up to three grounded observations, identifying human versus agent/tool actions.
3. What to improve: up to three changes, each with evidence, expected benefit, and validation method.
4. Optional prompt rewrite or scoped instruction/skill diff. Preserve the person's intent and original requirements.
5. Comparison, if supported: conditions, all observed outcomes, quality floor, metrics, and limits. Otherwise say what would need to be measured.
6. One next experiment; cost/time estimates are labeled as estimates.
7. Optional share-draft invitation, after the useful report is complete.

Avoid generic grades, motivational filler, harsh personality judgments, and forced positives. A short task needs a short report. A person may prefer no sharing invitations in later runs; respect that local setting.

## Public improvement story

Public stories are generalized accounts of learning, not copies of prompts/workflows or confidential work portfolios. Target 150–300 readable words plus up to three KPI tiles. The exact schema controls maximum field lengths.

Story structure:

- **Title:** a meaningful change or lesson. Example: “Checking assumptions before drafting reduced rework.”
- **Problem:** enough abstract context for a reader to recognize a similar task, with no project fingerprints.
- **What changed:** describe the intervention at a general level, such as defining acceptance criteria or separating source gathering from synthesis. Do not publish a proprietary step-by-step workflow.
- **Result:** a supported positive, neutral, negative, or unknown outcome. Separate measured values from estimates and anecdotes.
- **Lesson:** one general action another person can test.
- **Limits:** task conditions, missing evidence, sample coverage, and when the lesson might not transfer.

The first KPI is the prespecified primary metric for any structured improvement claim. A rounded equality is insufficient for a public numerical gain.

Include controlled comparison tags and skill evidence: task family, problem, objective, difficulty/basis, platform(s), model tier, intervention categories, demonstrated skills, and metric/rubric versions. Do not force an author to identify their actual job or employer; persona labels are optional, broad, self-selected task perspectives.

Example KPI display: “Model-token index: 100 → 75 (rounded), quality floor met.” Explain whether this came from local capture or self-report. Never silently turn it into “25% money saved.” Public exact underlying counts remain excluded for ordinary work-derived stories.

The client does not determine public trust badges. The server attaches submission origin and evidence tier. An author can edit the public narrative, but changes invalidate approval and must be rescanned. Unsupported narrative claims cannot overrule structured data; rewrite or block them.

## Make a story worth reading

Lead with a concrete lesson. Explain why the change might help and give a boundary where it might fail. Preserve interesting causally relevant structure, such as whether a task involved conflicting sources or repeat corrections, while removing identifying details. If meaningful context cannot be separated from confidentiality, do not publish a story.

Synthetic examples may teach a pattern but must carry a visible “Synthetic example” label everywhere, including cards, profile views, search, shared images, and exports. They are excluded from live improvement counts, benchmarks, badges, and candidate matching. Never invent a customer success story to fill an empty feed.

## Turn public learning into a local jump-start

When a reader chooses “Try this lesson,” return a short generic suggestion with source link, intended task/problem, conditions, and one measurable check. Let the reader adapt it on their laptop. Do not copy original proprietary prompts or auto-apply the source author's instructions.

If the source is not opted into automated community recommendations, it remains human-readable in ordinary browse/search but is not injected into automated coaching. Any downstream LLM summary must be grounded in allowed public sources and must not fabricate cross-user success rates.
